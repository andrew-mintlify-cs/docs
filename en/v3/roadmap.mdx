## Adding different types of capybaras

Capybaras belong to the genus *Hydrochoerus*, in the family Caviidae. There are two extant species. Use the sections below when adding a new capybara to your project.

<img src="/images/capybaras/river.jpg" alt="Capybara wading in a river" />

### Greater capybara (*Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris*)

The greater capybara is the largest living rodent in the world, native to most of South America east of the Andes. Adults reach roughly 106–134 cm in length and 35–66 kg in weight, with coarse brown fur, a barrel-shaped body, partially webbed feet, and small ears set high on the head. They are highly social, living in groups of 10–20 (occasionally up to 100 around water), and are semi-aquatic grazers that feed mainly on grasses and aquatic plants.

<img src="/images/capybaras/sitting.jpg" alt="Greater capybara resting on grass" />

### Lesser capybara (*Hydrochoerus isthmius*)

The lesser capybara is the smaller of the two species, found from eastern Panama through western Colombia and northwestern Venezuela — the northernmost range of any living capybara. Adults grow to about 90 cm and up to 28 kg, with a noticeably darker brown coat than their larger cousin. They inhabit marshes, ponds, and lagoons, travel in groups of around 20, and breed year-round in the water.

### Choosing a species

| Trait | Greater capybara | Lesser capybara |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific name | *Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris* | *Hydrochoerus isthmius* |
| Adult length | 106–134 cm | up to 90 cm |
| Adult weight | 35–66 kg | up to 28 kg |
| Range | South America, east of the Andes | Panama, western Colombia, western Venezuela |
| Coat | Reddish to grayish brown | Darker brown |
